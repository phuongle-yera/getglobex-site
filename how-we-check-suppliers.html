How we check suppliers

Short answer: GetGlobex (getglobex.com) checks public US customs and import records to see whether a supplier has actually shipped to US buyers in goods related to the product you describe (add an HS code if you know it). That is a signal, not a certification. You still do your own due diligence before you buy.

Inputs

What data we use

Our AI matching ranks suppliers from a small set of sources. We tell you which source each signal comes from.

  1. Public US customs and import records

    Shipment-level import data (for example, bill of lading records) that shows a supplier has shipped goods to US importers. We look at product description and HS context, origin, and how recent the shipments are.

  2. Company-provided information

    What a supplier tells us in their free profile — products, origin, capacity, contact. We label this as self-reported. We don’t treat it as proof on its own.

  3. HS codes on the match request

    The HS codes you bring with your match request, if you add them. Have an HS code? Add it. If you’re not sure, look it up in the official US Harmonized Tariff Schedule at hts.usitc.gov (USITC). We match against the codes you give us.

Plain meaning

What “already ships to the US” means — and doesn’t

When we say a supplier already ships to the US, we mean public US import records show they have shipped goods related to the product (and HS context, if provided) to US buyers. That is a signal, not a certification. It doesn’t say anything about product quality, factory conditions, certifications, or whether they’ll meet your specs and terms.

Honest limits

What we don’t claim

What we do

  • Check public US import records for shipment history related to the product
  • Separate record-based signals from self-reported profile info
  • Rank a shortlist and make a free matched intro
  • Tell you when we don’t have enough signal for the product yet

What we don’t claim

  • We don’t certify, audit, or vet suppliers, and we don’t inspect factories
  • No guarantee of product quality, delivery, or pricing
  • No guarantee of compliance with US product, safety, labeling, or customs rules
  • No fit scores, ratings, or reviews we can’t back with a source
  • We’re not a trading company and don’t take title to goods

Your side

You do your own due diligence

A shortlist is a starting point. Before you place an order, buyers typically:

  • Ask for samples and check them against your specs
  • Confirm the supplier’s legal entity, documents, and references
  • Arrange any inspection or testing you need, with a provider you choose
  • Confirm the HS classification, duties, and import requirements with a licensed customs broker or the official sources
  • Agree payment terms, Incoterms, and contracts directly with the supplier
