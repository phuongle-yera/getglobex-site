Supplier directory vs AI matching: what's the difference?

By GetGlobex team (getglobex.com) · Published 29 Oct 2026

Short answer: A supplier directory or catalog marketplace lists sellers and lets you search and filter what they say about themselves. AI supplier matching starts from the product you need (add your HS code if you know it) and shortlists the few suppliers whose public US customs and import records show they already ship related goods to US buyers. On GetGlobex (getglobex.com), buyers and suppliers deal directly, with no paid ranking.

The short answer

Both tools help you find suppliers, but they start from opposite ends. A directory starts from the sellers: it collects listings and lets you browse them. AI supplier matching starts from you: you describe the product you import, and the system looks for suppliers whose shipment history lines up with it.

Here is the difference in one line: "Catalogs list millions of sellers. GetGlobex shortlists the few already shipping your product to the US."

Neither approach is wrong. They do different jobs, and plenty of US importers use both at different points. The rest of this guide explains what each one does, where the evidence comes from, and how to choose.

What a supplier directory does

A supplier directory (some are run as large catalog marketplaces) is a searchable list of companies that sell goods. You type a product name, apply filters such as country or product type, and scroll through the results. Each listing is a profile page with photos, product lines, a company description and a way to make contact.

Listings are written by the sellers themselves

Most of what you read in a directory comes from the supplier. The seller chooses which products to show, how to describe its factory, which markets it says it serves and which photos to post. Some directories add their own badges or checks, and what those mean varies from site to site. The starting point, though, is self-description. That is useful for learning what a company says it can make. It tells you less about what it has actually shipped, and to whom.

How results get ordered

Directories order results with search relevance and filters. On many sites, placement can also be influenced by paid listings or advertising packages, so the first page of results may reflect who paid for visibility as well as who fits your need. That isn't hidden or improper. It is simply the business model of many catalogs, and it's worth knowing when you read a results page.

When a directory is the right tool

A directory is a good fit when you are:

  • browsing broadly to see what kinds of products and suppliers exist;
  • exploring a new category and want to learn the vocabulary, materials and options;
  • doing early research before you know exactly what you need, or which countries make it.

If you don't yet know what you want to buy, scrolling a large catalog can be the fastest way to find out.

What AI supplier matching does

AI supplier matching flips the starting point. Instead of you searching a catalog, you submit a need and get back a short, ranked list. On GetGlobex, "AI" means a ranked match built from shipment evidence and the details of your request. It does not mean software that negotiates for you, grades factories on quality or closes deals on its own. For the step-by-step, see how it works.

It starts from the product you need

You describe what you import in plain words: the product, materials, use and any specs that matter. Know your HS code? Add it. The HS code is the classification US customs uses for your product, so it helps narrow the match, but it isn't required. If you want to look yours up, the official US tariff schedule is at the USITC HTS search.

The evidence: public US customs and import records

Matching looks at public US customs and import records (for example, bill of lading records). These are shipment-level records that can show a supplier has shipped goods to US importers. That is a signal, not a certification. Short answers about what we use, and what we don't claim, are in our FAQ. If you'd like to read those records yourself, our guide on how to check if a supplier already ships to the US walks through it step by step.

How the shortlist is ranked

The shortlist is ranked on the signals in those records: the product description and HS context, the origin, and how recent the shipments are. Information a supplier gives in its own profile is labelled as self-reported and is not treated as proof on its own. There are no paid placements, and we don't publish fit scores, ratings or reviews we can't back with a source.

A short list, not a catalog to scroll through

The result is a handful of suppliers worth talking to, not thousands of listings. If there isn't enough signal for your product yet, we'll tell you rather than pad the list.

What the shipment evidence means and what it doesn't

When public US import records show that a supplier has shipped goods related to your product to US buyers, that tells you something real: the supplier has gone through the work of exporting to the United States in your kind of product. That is a signal, not a certification.

It does not tell you about product quality, factory conditions or certifications. GetGlobex doesn't certify, audit or vet suppliers, and we don't inspect factories. Shipment history doesn't guarantee delivery, compliance with US product, safety, labeling or customs rules, or that a supplier will meet your specs and terms.

So the shortlist is a starting point, not a finish line. Before you place an order, do your own diligence:

  • ask for samples and check them against your specs;
  • confirm the supplier's legal entity, documents and references;
  • arrange any inspection or testing you need, with a provider you choose;
  • confirm your HS classification, duties and import requirements with a licensed customs broker or the official sources.

Which should you use?

The honest answer is that it depends on where you are in your search.

Use a directory when…

  • you're still figuring out what product, material or design you want;
  • you want to see the full range of what's on offer before narrowing down;
  • you're researching a category you've never bought before.

Use AI matching when…

  • you know what you need, even if you can only describe it in plain words;
  • you want suppliers that public US import records show already ship related goods to US buyers;
  • you're a small or mid-size US importer without time to sift through thousands of listings;
  • you'd rather start from evidence than from self-written profiles.

Using both together

Many importers combine the two. Use a directory to learn the category and spot names, then use matching to get a short list grounded in US shipment history. You can also check any supplier you found in a directory against public import records yourself; this how-to guide explains how.
