How to find plastic packaging & household product suppliers for US import

By GetGlobex team (getglobex.com) · Published 5 Nov 2026

The short answer

Start from the product you need — bottles, jars, caps and lids, kitchenware or another plastic article — then look for suppliers worldwide whose public US customs and import records show they already ship that kind of product to the US. GetGlobex (getglobex.com) is an AI supplier-matching platform that shortlists that way for any product. Have an HS code? Add it.

What this category covers

On GetGlobex, Plastic packaging & household products covers:

  • Packaging — boxes, bags, bottles, jars, caps, lids and other closures and containers (often traded under HS 3923);
  • Kitchen and tableware / household plastics (often HS 3924);
  • Other plastic articles used in and around the home (often HS 3926).

Describe the product first. The HS code is optional. Have an HS code? Add it. If you want to look one up, use the official US tariff schedule at the USITC HTS search.

Ways US importers find suppliers

Importers typically mix product catalogs and directories, trade shows, sourcing trips and AI matching. Catalogs list many sellers and let you search what they say about themselves. Matching starts from the product you need and shortlists suppliers whose public US import records show related shipments. Read supplier directory vs AI matching for the difference. How to check a supplier already ships to the US

Public US customs and import records (for example, bill of lading records) can show whether a supplier has shipped related goods to US buyers. That is a signal, not a certification. It does not mean the supplier is certified, audited or inspected, and it says nothing about product quality or whether they will meet your specs.

Before you order

A shortlist is a starting point. Before you place an order, buyers typically:

  • Write a clear spec (size, neck finish, colour, decoration) and name the material you need when you know it (for example PET, HDPE or PP);
  • Confirm volumes, closures (caps, lids, pumps) and any labelling or decoration needs;
  • Ask for samples and check them against your spec;
  • Check references, and arrange any inspection or testing with a provider you choose;
  • Confirm HS classification, duties and import requirements with a licensed customs broker or official sources — including whether a US trade agreement can lower normal duty on qualifying goods (other US tariffs may still apply).

We don't publish food-contact or material-compliance rules here. Use official sources and your own advisors for those.
